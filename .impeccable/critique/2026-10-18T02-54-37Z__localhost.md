## Design Specificity Verdict

**Predominantly category-interchangeable, with a small set of genuinely authored moves the composition never uses.**

Category-interchangeable: the hero split (kicker, name-as-heading, capability sentence, two CTAs, masked portrait); `.section-label` mono eyebrows on five sections; six equal project cards where the featured one differs only by a 2px gradient border, a gradient pill and a `★`; the 27-logo skills wall; collapsed certificate list; contact form beside social links; a two-icon footer.

Authored and under-used: the `SMCurbelo/>` wordmark (script/stencil face resolved with a terminal prompt glyph); the teal token system (`#147568` / `#2dd4bf`, accent-tinted focus shadow); the About collage; the Nora OS light/dark image pair; a genuinely first-class bilingual implementation with real Rioplatense voseo.

The missed specificity is concrete: nothing on the page looks like what he builds. A modular Electron app with a plugin system, local AI and real cryptography is represented by a centred logo on a gradient inside a 16:10 box.

Renewal note: this verdict does not call for replacing the world. The palette, wordmark, collage and type scale earn renewal. What is missing is a work-led composition.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The first screen leads with the person, not the work
**What**: `Hero.tsx` renders a kicker, the h1 "I'm Sergio.", one sentence, and two buttons. The only visual is an `aria-hidden` portrait that is `display: none` below 580px. No work artifact in the first viewport at 1440x900, and none at all at 390x844. `#projects` begins ~3800px into a 6435px page.
**Why it matters**: owner requirement #1, unmet. Both a recruiter and a small-business owner must scroll past a portrait, a biography, a one-entry Experience band, 27 logos and 7 certificate rows before seeing anything he built.
**Fix**: make the hero's dominant visual the Nora OS application frame or a client-work strip; demote the portrait to a supporting slot; rewrite the h1 as the offer with the name as a smaller identity line; keep exactly two doors in the viewport.
**Suggested command**: /impeccable shape

### [P1] A placeholder ships inside the proof section
**What**: `Projects.json` id "04" points at `/images/clinica.svg`, whose entire content is "Dental Clinic" over the literal text "Replace with project screenshot" on a `#1a1a2e` rectangle. Visible second row, centre, desktop and mobile.
**Why it matters**: it sits in the section that establishes credibility and directly contradicts two confirmed product principles ("Keep it real", "Show, never claim"). A technical lead who catches it re-reads every other claim with suspicion.
**Fix**: replace with a real artifact (endpoint map, DAO/MVC layer diagram, terminal capture), or cut the card and fold the project into a one-line "Also built" list.
**Suggested command**: /impeccable polish

### [P1] The strongest evidence is hidden; the weakest is exposed
**What**: Nora OS highlights render only when expanded, and its description is clamped to 3 lines. Its proof-of-craft facts are one click deep inside a card the same size as "SpaceX Launches". Meanwhile `Project.category` is typed in `src/types/project.ts`, populated for all six projects, and rendered nowhere.
**Why it matters**: the grid says "six things of equal magnitude" when the truth is one production-grade desktop system plus five supporting projects. It discards the free signal that would let a client spot "E-commerce" at a glance.
**Fix**: give Nora OS a grid-spanning block with highlights visible by default and no truncation; treat the other five as a tighter list; render `category` as the card's primary descriptor; default `expandedId` to the featured id.
**Suggested command**: /impeccable layout

### [P1] The client door and the CV are not findable or actionable fast
**What**: nav is recruiter-shaped with no CV entry and no client entry. "Download CV" exists exactly once, inside About, ~1.2 screens down. The footer is two icons and a copyright. Certificate PDFs live in 7 collapsed rows with no marker showing which 5 have one. Nothing says he takes on small-business work.
**Why it matters**: owner requirements #3 and #4. The highest-value action for a recruiter is buried; the highest-value recognition moment for a client requires parsing truncated prose because `category` is unrendered.
**Fix**: persistent CV control in nav and footer; split the two doors explicitly on the first screen; render `category`; mark PDF-bearing rows and close the UTU row's dangling date with "Present"/"Actualidad".
**Suggested command**: /impeccable clarify

### [P2] The contact section is the weakest-supported moment on the page
**What**: the email address never appears as selectable text (only inside the error string and behind a mailto labelled "Email"); no response-time or scope expectation above the button; `aria-invalid` set on all three fields on any failure; no `autoComplete` on visible inputs; success and error both self-destruct after 3.5s.
**Why it matters**: every visitor who crosses the decision threshold lands here, and editing friction plus a self-destructing confirmation raise abandonment exactly where it costs most. The misattributed invalid state is a real accessibility defect.
**Fix**: print the email as selectable text in the contact intro and footer; add `autoComplete`/`inputMode`; move an expectation line above the button; set the error at form level; keep the success banner until the next submit.
**Suggested command**: /impeccable harden

## Minor Observations

1. `--font-mono` names JetBrains Mono and Fira Code; neither is loaded (only Sora is), so every eyebrow renders in the visitor's OS monospace.
2. The 27 tech icons are hot-linked from `cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/devicons/devicon@latest/...` — a mutable ref with no width/height and no fallback.
3. Dead CSS with no JSX consumer: `.overlay`, `.modal`, `.modal-content`, `.close-button`, `.pdf-viewer`, `@keyframes fadeIn`/`scaleIn`.
4. `.about > img` applies a box shadow and a mask to a cut-out collage, producing a faint rectangular halo around the subject.
5. Three simultaneous signals for one fact on the featured card: gradient border, gradient pill, and a `★` glyph.
6. The featured thumbnail pair is unequal: dark `nora_os.webp` reads as a product; light `nora_os_alt.webp` is a small mark in a large gradient field.
7. `index.html` hard-codes `lang="en"`; `LanguageContext` corrects it after hydration, so non-JS crawlers and social unfurls always see English with one generic `og:image`.
8. ES course date strings are identical to EN, and `'UTU, Uruguay · 2023 –'` has a trailing dash with no endpoint.
9. `.switch .slider::before { background-color: white }` and `--text-on-accent: #ffffff` are hard-coded light-theme values outside the tinted-surface logic.

Capture artifact, not a defect: the full-page captures show a full-width accent bar at the very top — that is `.scroll-progress` stitched at `scaleX(1)`, not a layout bug.
